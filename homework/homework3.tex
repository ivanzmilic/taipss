{\bf Problem 1:} Building on the hands-on 6\&7, conduct the following numerical calculations/experiments, to investigate 3D simulation of convection in more detail (\emph{Note:} some of these calculations were already covered in the lecture).

\emph{Important:} Please do not turn in jupyter notebook, but rather a textual report, in pdf format or similar.  

\begin{itemize}
  \item Try to define the surface of the simulated atmosphere. Discuss the result. 

  \item Assuming the atmosphere is composed of pure hydrogen (it is not, but for simplicity assume this), solve the equation of state for one fixed $x$ and $y$, and different $z$. That is, using Saha equation and results of hands-on 5, estimate the mass density from the values of temperature and pressure. Note that the pressure provided in the simulation is in CGS units!


  \item Repeat this calculation for the whole atmosphere. \emph{Alternatively: choose a reasonable number of mean molecular mass to find mass density from the pressure and temperature without solving for equation of state expliclty}.

  \item Calculate the kinetic energy flux density, and mean kinetic energy flux density (averaged over $x$ and $y$). Plot mean kinetic energy flux density as a function of $z$. Recall that kinetic energy flux density is: 

  \begin{equation}
  F_kin = \frac{\rho v^2}{2} v_z
  \end{equation}

  \item From the fluctuations of temperature and momentum density, calculate the entalphy flux density and mean enthalpy flux density

  \begin{equation}
  F_{ent} = (\rho v_z - <\rho v_z>_{x,y}) C_P (T-<T>_{x,y})
  \end{equation}

  Where for $C_P$ you can take the value 21 kJ / kg/ K.

  \item Analyze the behavior of mean enthalpy flux density and mean kinetic flux density, as well as their sum , with height. What can you conclude? 

\end{itemize}
